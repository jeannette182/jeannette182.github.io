About

My current research looks at how Africa's net-zero transition is financed, and at what decides whether capital actually reaches climate investment. I came to this from statistics and mathematics: my Master's at AIMS Rwanda focused on predicting rainy-season onset with machine learning, and I have worked on tax policy modelling with UNU-WIDER, on risk analytics at the Rwanda Revenue Authority, and on official statistics at the National Institute of Statistics of Rwanda.

I am interested in AI for climate, weather prediction, climate finance and climate resilience in Africa, and I am open to research collaborations.

Research

Current · Research Fellowship, AIMS RIC

Financing Africa's Net-Zero Transition: An Integrated Systems Framework of Climate Finance, Green Bonds, and Carbon Markets

A PRISMA 2020-guided systematic literature review of how financial barriers, institutional conditions and financing mechanisms affect whether capital reaches climate-investment opportunities in Africa.

The work combines Soft Systems Methodology and Financial Integration Theory to separate financial availability from effective connectivity. It organises the evidence in an ontology-based knowledge graph that links financing constraints, institutions, financial mechanisms, investment processes and climate outcomes.

Guarantees · Blended finance · Green bonds · Adaptation finance · Article 6 carbon markets

2022–2024 · UNU-WIDER

A tax-benefit microsimulation model for Rwanda

Co-developed Rwanda's tax-benefit microsimulation model with the UNU-WIDER research team, covering the documentation of tax and benefit legislation, preparation of survey-based input data, the feasibility study and implementation in EUROMOD. The model assesses the distributional and socio-economic impacts of policy reforms.

Read the working paper on the 2023–2024 income tax reform

2021 · Master's thesis, AIMS Rwanda

Predicting the Onset of the MAM Rainy Season in Musanze District, Rwanda Using Machine Learning

I used Python to analyse CHIRPS rainfall and NASA climate datasets, comparing Gradient Boosting, Random Forest and Multilayer Perceptron models for predicting the start of the March–May rains.
